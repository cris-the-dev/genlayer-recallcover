How it works

The regulator's page decides.

  1. I
    Open

    An underwriter names the product, the regulator's recall page, the premium, the payout and the number of policies, and escrows payout × policies. Validators read the page: no recall of the product yet.

  2. II
    Buy

    Buyers pay the premium. Buying reads the page too: once a recall is listed, no cover is sold and the premium goes straight back.

  3. III
    Watch

    Anyone can ask validators to read the page again. They agree on none or recalled, and on a sentence quoted from the page that each of them finds in their own copy.

  4. IV
    Settle

    Recalled: anyone sends each holder its payout. No recall by the end: one last reading, then the underwriter gets the collateral back and keeps the premiums.

Built for both sides

For buyers

  • The payout is already in escrow when you buy: the underwriter cannot fail to pay.
  • A recall on the regulator's page is enough: you don't file a claim or prove a loss.
  • Shops stuck with recalled stock, owners of a recalled appliance: one fixed sum, at once.
  • Buying after a recall is listed is refused and refunded in the same transaction.

For underwriters

  • Price recall risk per product, sell cover to anyone, keep every premium if nothing happens.
  • Your exposure is capped at the collateral you chose.
  • Collateral of unsold policies comes back whatever happens.
  • No claims desk, no disputes: the regulator's page settles it.

FAQ

Who decides that a product is recalled?

GenLayer validators. Each one fetches the regulator's page itself and answers one question: does it announce a recall of this product? A result counts only when they agree, and a recall also needs a sentence from the page that every validator finds.

What if the page is down?

The reading reverts and nothing changes; try again later. A page that is down is never taken as “no recall”. If it cannot be read for 30 days after the end, the pool can be released without a reading.

What does the underwriter get, and when?

No recall by the end: all the collateral back plus every premium, after one last reading. Recall: the premiums and the collateral of unsold policies, at once; holders' payouts stay for them.

Can a rumour or an investigation trigger payouts?

No. Only a page that announces a recall, withdrawal, stop-sale or do-not-use notice of this product. Validators answer “none” for anything less, and the quote must be on the page.

Who sends the payouts?

Anyone: each policy's payout goes to the address that bought it, whoever presses the button.
